How a Mechanical Watch Works
Escape wheel
The escape wheel is part of the escapement mechanism, which is responsible for controlling the release of energy from the mainspring (the power source) to the gear train, and ultimately to the hands of the watch. The escape wheel engages with the pallet fork (another component in the escapement), which helps regulate the flow of energy at regular intervals.
Second wheel
Is part of the gear system that ensures the second hand of the watch moves correctly, typically in one-second intervals. Its movement is regulated by the escapement system, ensuring that the watch runs accurately and smoothly. The second wheel is vital for translating the energy from the mainspring into the visible motion of the seconds hand.
Third wheel
The third wheel acts as a conduit for the transfer of power between the second and fourth wheels. It is responsible for ensuring that the minute hand moves correctly and at the right speed (one full rotation every hour). Without the third wheel, the energy wouldn't be properly transmitted through the gear train to keep the minute hand moving, thus disrupting the overall timekeeping of the watch.
Center wheel
The center wheel is the first wheel in the watch's gear train and plays a fundamental role in transferring power from the mainspring to the rest of the movement. Its movement drives the second wheel, which continues the energy flow to the other wheels, ultimately making the watch hands move. Without the center wheel, the energy from the mainspring wouldn’t reach the rest of the watch, and the timekeeping wouldn’t function properly.
Mainspring barrel
The mainspring barrel serves as the energy storage unit for a mechanical watch. It houses the mainspring and controls how that stored energy is released gradually to power the watch’s movement. Without the mainspring barrel, the watch wouldn’t be able to store and manage energy efficiently, and the watch would not function properly. The barrel and mainspring are fundamental to the mechanics of any traditional mechanical watch, ensuring the flow of energy is smooth and controlled, resulting in accurate timekeeping.
Crown wheel
The crown wheel is a small but important gear that enables the winding of the watch by transferring the rotational energy from the crown to the mainspring, storing energy for the watch's movement
Ratchet wheel
The ratchet wheel is essential for the proper winding of a mechanical watch. It works with the pawl to allow the mainspring to be wound and store energy without it unwinding accidentally. This ensures the watch keeps running smoothly without the mainspring losing tension prematurely
Ratchet wheel click
The click is a small, spring-loaded part that interacts with the ratchet wheel in the winding mechanism of a mechanical watch. It engages with the teeth of the ratchet wheel to ensure the mainspring is properly wound and stores energy without allowing it to unwind prematurely. The distinctive clicking sound you hear when winding your watch is the result of the click locking into place with each turn of the crown.
Bearings
Bearings in mechanical watches are crucial for minimizing friction between rotating parts, which helps in improving efficiency, reducing wear, and increasing the longevity of the movement. The most common type is jewel bearings, which use synthetic rubies to ensure smooth and precise operation. Bearings are especially important for the balance wheel and rotor (in automatic watches), where they help maintain accuracy and energy efficiency
